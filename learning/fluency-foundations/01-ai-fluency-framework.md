## Why AI Fluency, and three ways of engaging with AI

The course frames "fluency" as working with AI **effectively, efficiently,
ethically, and safely** — not a technical skill so much as a set of habits.
It introduces three distinct ways people actually engage with AI systems:

- **Automation** — AI completes a specific task based on your instructions.
- **Augmentation** — you and AI collaborate as thinking and task-execution
  partners.
- **Agency** — you configure AI to work more independently on your behalf,
  shaping its knowledge and behavior patterns rather than issuing one-off
  instructions.

The 4D competencies below apply across all three modes — the same
Delegation/Description/Discernment/Diligence judgment shows up whether
you're automating a single task or setting up an agent to run more
independently.

## The 4D Framework

**Delegation** — deciding what work to do yourself versus with AI, based on
three components: **Problem Awareness** (understanding your actual goal and
the work involved), **Platform Awareness** (knowing what a given AI system
can and can't do), and **Task Delegation** itself (strategically dividing
the work). Effective delegation needs both domain expertise and an
understanding of AI's capabilities — the goal isn't automating everything,
it's building the most effective partnership for a given task.

**Description** — communicating your intent clearly, broken into three
types: **Product Description** (what output you want — format, audience,
scope), **Process Description** (how you want the AI to approach the task),
and **Performance Description** (how you want it to behave during the
collaboration — concise or detailed, challenging or supportive). The
course's underlying point: AI can't read your mind, and result quality
often comes down to how clearly you communicated up front.

**Discernment** — evaluating what comes back, again in three parts:
**Product Discernment** (is the output itself accurate and appropriate?),
**Process Discernment** (did the AI reason soundly to get there?), and
**Performance Discernment** (was its behavior during the collaboration
actually helpful?). Discernment and Description work as a continuous
feedback loop — you describe, evaluate, refine, and describe again.

**Diligence** — using AI responsibly, in three parts: **Creation Diligence**
(being thoughtful about which AI system you use and how), **Transparency
Diligence** (being honest about AI's role in your work with whoever needs to
know), and **Deployment Diligence** (taking ownership of anything you
ultimately share, verified and vouched for as if you'd made it yourself).
Expectations for disclosure vary by context — personal, academic,
professional — but the underlying responsibility doesn't.

## Deep Dive: what generative AI actually is, and where it breaks

Generative AI creates new content rather than just analyzing existing data.
Three developments made today's large models possible: the transformer
architecture (an algorithmic breakthrough), vast amounts of training data,
and large increases in computational power. Training itself happens in two
stages — **pre-training** (learning patterns across billions of examples)
and **fine-tuning** (learning to follow instructions and respond helpfully).

Real capabilities include versatility across language tasks, maintaining
conversational context, and switching between very different kinds of tasks
without retraining. Real limitations include a knowledge cutoff date,
hallucination (fluent but factually wrong output), context window limits,
and genuine difficulty with some kinds of complex reasoning. The course's
framing: the most effective use combines human judgment, creativity, and
ethical oversight with AI's complementary strengths — neither replaces the
other.

## Effective prompting techniques

The course's practical Description toolkit — six techniques: **give
context** (what you want and why), **show examples** of the output style
you're after, **specify constraints** (format, length, other requirements),
**break complex tasks into steps**, **ask the AI to think first** before
answering, and **define its role or tone**. One extra technique the course
calls out specifically: you can ask the AI itself to help improve your
prompt — genuinely one of the more useful, underused moves available.
Prompting is treated as iterative by design — expect to refine based on
results rather than getting it right in one shot.

## Applying the framework to a real project

Two lessons have you carry one real project through the whole framework: a
project-planning exercise (pick a medium-sized project, work out with Claude
which parts benefit from your expertise versus AI's strengths) followed by a
"Description-Discernment loop" exercise where you actually execute that
project, describing what you need, evaluating what comes back, and refining
across each task until you're satisfied — the 4Ds moving from concept to
practiced habit.

## Conclusion

The closing lesson ties the 4Ds back to the three ways of engaging with AI
(Automation, Augmentation, Agency) and makes one point explicitly: fluency
develops through practice, not overnight mastery. AI systems are powerful
but not magic — they're only as useful and safe as the judgment brought to
them.

## Additional activities (self-paced, after finishing the course)

The course closes with optional follow-on practice: building a personal AI
fluency development plan, building a personal library of prompts/patterns
for tasks you do often, and a set of "Game Night" exercises (riddles,
cooperative crosswords, word association, twenty questions, collaborative
storytelling) designed specifically to practice Description and Discernment
in a low-stakes, playful way.

## Reflection / practice prompts

- Pick a task you delegated to Claude recently. Walk it through all 4 Ds:
  was delegation the right call, was your description clear, how did you
  discern the output's quality, and what diligence did you apply before
  using it?
- Of Product, Process, and Performance Discernment, which do you find
  hardest to apply — and why might that be?
- Try the "ask the AI to improve your own prompt" technique on something
  you're about to ask Claude for. Compare the revised prompt's result to
  what you would have gotten with the original.
- Draft one sentence of your own "diligence statement" for a real piece of
  AI-assisted work — which AI system, how it contributed, and what you did
  to verify the result.
